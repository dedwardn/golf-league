# S8 Pairing proposals

**Implemented**

A round's pairings can now be proposed from the season table. Swiss ranks every player by
standings, index then id, settles the single-sided pairing first by rotation, and pairs
the rest from the top, preferring an opponent they have not met and otherwise the one met
least recently. The two team kinds build sides first, `high_low` inward from the ranked
order and `random` from a seeded shuffle, then pair those sides by combined points. The
admin edits the result; publishing is what makes it a fact.

127 tests, 100 percent coverage of `league.domain`. 414 added lines across two new files,

Every accept criterion has a test: round 1 ordered by index, a shared position broken by
index then id, the highest-ranked unmet opponent, the least recent rematch when all are
met, the highest-ranked of those last met in the same round, opposite best ball sides
counted as having met, the single-sided player chosen
before the rest are paired, the rotation to the lowest-ranked with the fewest and its
restart from the bottom, a player absent from a round still eligible, `high_low` with the
middle player alone, `random` reproducible from `rng_seed` with the last of the shuffle
alone, sides paired by combined points, the one-member side taking the single-sided
pairing, and the lowest side taking it when there is no one-member side.

The three functions have no caller outside tests. S14 is what loads the season, picks a
kind and publishes the result.

**Public API**

```python
# pairing.py
def swiss_pairings(
    standings: Sequence[StandingsRow],
    index_tenths: Mapping[str, int],
    history: Sequence[Sequence[Pairing]],
) -> tuple[Pairing, ...]
```

A swiss proposal of single sides for every player in `standings`, in pairing order with
the single-sided pairing last. `history` is this season's published pairings: one inner
sequence per earlier round, oldest round first, holding every pairing of that round's
duel contests together. Only its position is read, because R11 asks which meeting was the
least recent, not which round number it fell in. No error path.

```python
# pairing.py
def high_low_pairings(
    standings: Sequence[StandingsRow], index_tenths: Mapping[str, int]
) -> tuple[Pairing, ...]
```

A team proposal whose sides join the highest-ranked with the lowest-ranked and so on
inward, the middle player alone with an odd number of players. No history: sides are new
each round. No error path.

```python
# pairing.py
def random_pairings(
    standings: Sequence[StandingsRow], index_tenths: Mapping[str, int], rng_seed: int
) -> tuple[Pairing, ...]
```

The same, with sides taken as consecutive pairs of a `random.Random(rng_seed)` shuffle of
the ranked order, so the proposal is reproducible from the seed and the last player of
the shuffle is the one alone. No error path.

**Deviations from plan**

Two settled by the owner after the plan was approved, both already in SPEC:

1. The single-sided player is chosen before anyone is paired, not left to whoever the
   pass from the top leaves over. The approved plan said "with an odd number the last
   pairing is single-sided", which could hand it to a player who had just had one. R11
   now states the order, and `test_r11_the_odd_player_is_the_lowest_ranked_with_the_fewest_single_sided_pairings`
   asserts the whole tuple, so the leftover reading fails it: that reading gives
   `(ann,cam), (bob,dee), (eve alone)` where the rotation gives `(ann,cam), (bob,eve),
   (dee alone)`.
2. The rotation goes to the lowest-ranked player among those with the fewest published
   single-sided pairings, not to the lowest-ranked who has not had one. The plan proposed
   falling back to the lowest-ranked once everyone had had one, which would have given
   the bottom player the virtual side every round from then on.

Three from the shape of the code:

3. `_swiss` takes the index of the side that plays alone rather than computing it, and
   the two kinds choose it with `_swiss_alone` and `_team_alone`. The plan had one
   `_alone` inside the shared pass. R11's team change after validation makes the choice
   differ by kind: teams give it to the one-member side when there is one, swiss to the
   rotation. One pairing pass is still shared.
4. `_swiss` works over sides, not players, so a swiss side of one and a team side of one
   or two go through the same code. The plan said this; it is recorded here because it is
   why `_opponent` keys on `frozenset(side + other)`, which for single sides is exactly
   R11's pair of players and for team sides is never found, the team kinds passing no
   history.
5. No `Side` type alias. `tuple[str, ...]` is written out, because `Side` is already a
   public name in `results.py` and means something else there: a net card with the players
   who earn what it wins.

Three in the tests, all from the owner's revised list:

6. The odd-side test uses 9 players, not the 7 the plan proposed. Seven players make
   three two-player sides and one one-member side, four in all, which is even, so it can
   never produce a single-sided pairing.
7. `test_r11_opposite_best_ball_sides_have_met` and
   `test_r11_once_everyone_has_had_one_the_rotation_starts_again_from_the_bottom` were
   added, and `test_r11_an_odd_number_of_two_player_sides_gives_the_lowest_side_the_single_sided_pairing`
   after the team change, so both branches of `_team_alone` are reached by a worked case
   rather than by coverage alone.
8. `test_r11_random_sides_are_reproducible_from_rng_seed` pins two seeds. Seed 2 is the
   case that drove the team change: it puts a one-member side above a two-player side on
   combined points, so it fails under "lowest-ranked side always".

Applied at audit:

9. `_team_pairings` holds the tail both team kinds shared: ordering the sides by combined
   points and running the pairing pass with `_team_alone`. `high_low_pairings` and
   `random_pairings` now only form their sides and call it. Audit finding 1.
10. `_swiss_alone` takes the ranked player ids rather than sides of one, so its key is
    `counts.get(order[place], 0)` instead of a sum over a single element. Its only caller
    builds those sides from the same order. Audit finding 3.
11. `_swiss` is now `_pair_from_the_top`. All three public functions call it, including
    the two team kinds, so a name that said "swiss" read as though `high_low_pairings`
    were running a swiss. `_swiss_alone` keeps its name: it really is swiss-only. Audit
    finding 7.
12. `test_r11_a_tie_on_least_recent_goes_to_the_highest_ranked` was added. Two duel
    contests in one round, or one best ball pairing, put two meetings at the same
    position, which R11 did not cover. R11 now states that the tie goes to the
    highest-ranked, which is what ordering the pool best first already did, so the rule
    caught up with the code rather than the other way round. Audit finding 6.
13. The second assertion in `test_r11_random_sides_are_reproducible_from_rng_seed` is
    gone. `Random(rng_seed)` is built inside the call, so once the first assertion pinned
    the tuple, calling twice and comparing could not fail. Audit finding 4.

Declined at audit:

14. Finding 2 asked for the `len(sides) % 2 == 0` guard to move out of `_swiss_alone` and
    `_team_alone` into their callers. Declined by the owner: after finding 1 there are
    still two call sites, so the check would move rather than shrink.

Settled at audit, no change:

15. Finding 5, a missing entry in `index_tenths` raising `KeyError` rather than
    `ValueError`. The owner settled it as a caller bug: S14 builds `standings` and
    `index_tenths` from the same seeded players, so the two cannot disagree, and
    ARCHITECTURE's `ValueError` convention is about input a rule could legitimately be
    handed.

Nothing else differs. The two planned files are the two files changed, the three planned
public functions are the three that exist, and no helper beyond those listed above.

**Deliberately not done**

- Any guard for a player in `standings` with no entry in `index_tenths`. Agreed with the
  owner: S14 builds both from the same seeded players, so it cannot arise there.
- Validating a proposal after the admin has edited it, and publishing. S14.
- Season-team pairings for a teambonus round. R11 leaves those to hand in version 1.
- Any link from a round's contest config to a pairing kind. Nothing in the domain says
  which kind a round proposes; S14 is where the admin picks.
- `RoundFacts`, and resolving `history` out of a season. S9.

**Open questions**

1. With 7 or 11 players a team round has a one-member side but an even number of sides,
   so no single-sided pairing exists and the lone player faces a best ball of two. The
   owner has recorded this as a limit of two-player sides at odd counts that the admin
   edits around. Nothing in the code marks it, so the admin has to notice it on the
   proposal screen. Worth a warning in S17, not a rule.
2. `_met` keeps only the most recent meeting of a pair, which is all R11's "least
   recently" needs now that a tie on it goes by rank. If a tiebreak ever wants how many
   times two players have met, that is a second index, not a change to this one.
3. `swiss_pairings` proposes for every player with a standings row, so every seeded player
   is in a pairing whether or not they intend to play that round. R9 already handles a
   player who does not hand in a card: their side is missing and the opponent meets the
   virtual side. S14 decides whether the admin is offered a shorter list.
